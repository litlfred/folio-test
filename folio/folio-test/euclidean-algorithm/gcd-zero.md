The "bounded above" argument above needs $a$ and $b$ not both zero: every
integer divides $0$, so the common divisors of $(0,0)$ are all of
$\mathbb{Z}$ and there is no largest.

The usual convention sets $\gcd(0,0) = 0$. This is not a patch over an
awkward case. Under the divisibility order — where $d \leq e$ means
$d \mid e$ — the greatest common divisor is the *meet*, and $0$ is the top
element of that order, not the bottom. Every statement in this chapter holds
with that reading, including $\gcd(a, 0) = |a|$.

Reading "greatest" as "largest under $\le$ on $\mathbb{Z}$" and reading it as
"greatest under divisibility" agree everywhere except at $(0,0)$, which is
why the convention looks arbitrary if you only ever hold the first reading.
